# Digital Forensics and Evidence

## Purpose

Digital forensics applies systematic methods to identify, preserve, collect, examine, analyze, and report digital evidence.

## Evidence Sources

Potential sources include disk images, memory, logs, network captures, cloud records, mobile devices, application artifacts, authentication records, and security-tool telemetry.

## Evidence Integrity

Use appropriate acquisition methods, hashes, documentation, access controls, and secure storage to demonstrate that evidence was not improperly altered.

## Chain of Custody

Chain of custody records who collected, transferred, stored, accessed, or otherwise handled evidence. It establishes accountability and supports evidence integrity.

## Order of Volatility

More volatile information should generally be collected before less volatile information when circumstances permit. Memory and active network state can disappear quickly, while data stored on persistent media generally remains available longer.

## Forensic Reporting

Document collection methods, timestamps, tools, evidence identifiers, hashes where appropriate, observations, analysis, limitations, and conclusions. Separate facts and observations from assumptions.

## Legal Considerations

Evidence handling may have legal, privacy, contractual, or regulatory requirements. Investigators should follow organizational procedures and involve appropriate legal or incident-response personnel.

## Exam Focus

Preservation protects evidence from alteration or loss. Chain of custody documents possession and handling. Hashes can help demonstrate file or image integrity.
