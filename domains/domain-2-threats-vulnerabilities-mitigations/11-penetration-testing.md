# Penetration Testing

## 1. Purpose

Penetration testing is an authorized security assessment in which testers attempt to demonstrate the practical impact of vulnerabilities under controlled conditions. The objective is to improve security, not to cause uncontrolled damage.

## 2. Rules of Engagement

Before testing begins, the organization and testing team should define authorization, scope, targets, exclusions, permitted techniques, testing windows, communication procedures, data-handling requirements, emergency contacts, and stopping conditions.

## 3. Testing Perspectives

### Black Box
The tester starts with little or no internal information, approximating an external attacker.

### White Box
The tester receives extensive information such as architecture, source code, or credentials. This enables deeper testing.

### Gray Box
The tester receives some internal information or access, representing a partially informed attacker or user.

## 4. Testing Areas

Penetration testing can include network, web application, API, wireless, cloud, mobile, physical, and social-engineering assessments depending on scope and authorization.

## 5. Vulnerability Validation

A penetration tester attempts to determine whether a reported weakness is practically exploitable and what impact could result. Testing should minimize data exposure and avoid unnecessary disruption.

## 6. Reporting

A professional report normally includes an executive summary, scope, methodology, findings, evidence, affected assets, risk or severity, business impact, remediation recommendations, limitations, and retest results.

## 7. Cleanup

Temporary accounts, test files, configurations, access paths, and other artifacts must be removed according to the rules of engagement. Sensitive test data must be handled securely.

## 8. Security+ Exam Focus

Penetration testing requires authorization. Vulnerability scanning is primarily identification; penetration testing validates vulnerabilities through controlled attempts. Rules of engagement establish boundaries before testing begins.

## 9. Key Takeaways

A penetration test is a controlled simulation, not unrestricted hacking. Legal authorization, careful scope, safety controls, evidence handling, reporting, and remediation are essential components.
