# Disaster Recovery and Incident Response Planning

## 1. Disaster Recovery

Disaster recovery (DR) focuses on restoring technology, systems, services, and data after a disruptive event. DR is a component of broader business continuity.

A DR plan should identify recovery priorities, responsible personnel, dependencies, communication methods, backup resources, restoration procedures, and recovery objectives.

## 2. Backup Strategy

Backup planning should consider frequency, retention, storage location, integrity, encryption, access control, and restoration testing. A backup that cannot be restored is not a reliable recovery mechanism.

Organizations should protect backups from ransomware and administrative compromise by using appropriate access controls, separation, immutability where appropriate, and independent recovery mechanisms.

## 3. Recovery Sequence

Recovery commonly progresses from assessing the event and activating the appropriate plan through restoring foundational infrastructure, identity and networking dependencies, critical applications, data, and business processes. The exact sequence depends on architecture and BIA results.

## 4. Incident Response Planning

Incident response defines how an organization prepares for, identifies, contains, eradicates, and recovers from security incidents. It also establishes communications, escalation, evidence handling, roles, and decision authority.

Common lifecycle models include preparation, detection and analysis, containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident activity.

## 5. Communications

Incident communications should distinguish technical responders, management, legal, privacy, public relations, affected users, customers, regulators, and law enforcement. Information should be shared according to authorization and need.

## 6. Lessons Learned

After an incident, organizations should document what happened, which controls worked or failed, the timeline, root or contributing causes, response gaps, and improvements. Lessons learned should result in actionable changes.

## 7. Security+ Exam Focus

Business continuity keeps important functions operating; disaster recovery restores systems and services; incident response manages security incidents. These disciplines overlap but have different objectives.

## Key Takeaways

Plans must be documented, assigned to responsible people, tested, updated, and aligned with business priorities. Preparation reduces decision-making delays during an actual event.
