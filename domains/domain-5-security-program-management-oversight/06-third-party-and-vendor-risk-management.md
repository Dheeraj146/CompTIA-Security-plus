# Third-Party and Vendor Risk Management

## 1. Overview

Organizations depend on vendors, cloud providers, contractors, software suppliers, managed service providers, and other third parties. Each dependency can introduce security, privacy, availability, compliance, and supply-chain risk.

## 2. Due Diligence and Due Care

**Due diligence** is the process of investigating and understanding risks before and during a relationship. **Due care** is taking reasonable actions to address known risks.

## 3. Vendor Assessment

Assessment may examine security architecture, access controls, incident response, vulnerability management, privacy practices, business continuity, subcontractors, data handling, and compliance evidence.

## 4. Contracts and Security Requirements

Contracts can define security requirements, breach notification expectations, data ownership, audit rights, service levels, data location, retention and destruction, access requirements, and responsibilities when the relationship ends.

## 5. Supply-Chain Risk

Software and hardware may contain components originating from many suppliers. Organizations should maintain visibility into dependencies, evaluate supplier security, verify provenance where possible, manage software components, and monitor material changes.

## 6. Vendor Lifecycle

Vendor risk management continues through **selection → assessment → contracting → onboarding → monitoring → reassessment → offboarding**.

Offboarding should revoke accounts and credentials, recover assets, terminate connectivity, remove access tokens, and address data return or destruction requirements.

## 7. Security+ Exam Focus

Third-party risk does not disappear because a service is outsourced. Contracts and technical controls should clearly establish responsibilities, and vendor risk should be monitored throughout the relationship.

## Key Takeaways

Third-party security is a shared organizational responsibility. Risk assessments, contractual controls, continuous monitoring, and secure offboarding are essential.
