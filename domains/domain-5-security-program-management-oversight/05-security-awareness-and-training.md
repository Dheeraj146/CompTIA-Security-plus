# Security Awareness and Training

## 1. Purpose

Security awareness programs reduce human-related security risk by teaching users how to recognize threats, follow organizational requirements, protect information, and report suspicious activity.

## 2. Awareness vs Training

Awareness creates broad understanding and reinforces secure behavior. Training develops the knowledge and skills required for a particular role or responsibility. Specialized personnel may require role-based training in addition to general awareness.

## 3. Common Topics

Programs commonly address phishing, credential protection, MFA, acceptable use, data handling, removable media, physical security, incident reporting, privacy, social engineering, remote work, and organizational policies.

## 4. Role-Based Training

Administrators, developers, executives, help-desk personnel, security analysts, and privileged users have different responsibilities and therefore require different security training. Developers may need secure coding practices, while administrators may need privileged-access and configuration-security training.

## 5. Measuring Effectiveness

Organizations can evaluate programs through participation rates, assessment results, phishing-simulation trends, reporting rates, incident metrics, and behavioral changes. The goal is to improve measurable security behavior rather than merely prove that training was completed.

## 6. Security+ Exam Focus

Security awareness is preventive, but it should be combined with technical and procedural controls. Users should have a clear, low-friction mechanism for reporting suspicious messages or security events.

## Key Takeaways

Effective awareness is continuous, role-appropriate, measurable, and supported by organizational culture and technical controls.
