# Business Impact Analysis and Continuity

## 1. Business Impact Analysis

A Business Impact Analysis (BIA) identifies how disruption to business processes, systems, applications, facilities, suppliers, and data would affect the organization over time.

The BIA helps determine which functions are most critical and what resources are required to restore them.

## 2. Recovery Objectives

**RTO (Recovery Time Objective)** is the targeted maximum amount of time to restore a service or process after disruption.

**RPO (Recovery Point Objective)** represents the targeted amount of data loss measured in time. For example, an RPO of one hour means the organization aims to recover data to a point no more than approximately one hour before the disruption.

**MTTR (Mean Time to Repair/Recover)** measures average restoration time. **MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures)** measures average operating time between failures for a repairable system.

## 3. Business Continuity

Business continuity keeps critical functions operating or restores them quickly during disruption. It includes people, processes, facilities, technology, communications, suppliers, and alternate operating arrangements.

## 4. Continuity Strategies

Examples include alternate sites, redundant systems, backup communication channels, remote-work capabilities, spare equipment, alternate suppliers, and manual workarounds.

An organization may use a hot site for rapid operational readiness, a warm site requiring some preparation, or a cold site requiring substantially more setup before operations can resume.

## 5. Dependencies

Continuity planning must consider upstream and downstream dependencies. A database may depend on storage, networking, identity services, power, DNS, cloud services, and specialized staff. Restoring one component does not necessarily restore the business process.

## 6. Testing

Continuity plans should be exercised regularly. Tabletop exercises validate decision-making and responsibilities; simulations and technical recovery tests provide progressively stronger evidence that procedures work.

## 7. Security+ Exam Focus

RTO concerns time to restore. RPO concerns acceptable data loss measured in time. BIA determines business impact and helps establish recovery priorities.

## Key Takeaways

Continuity planning connects technical recovery with actual business requirements. Recovery objectives should be derived from business impact rather than selected arbitrarily.
