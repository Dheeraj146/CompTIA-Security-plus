# Risk Management

## 1. Overview

Risk management is the structured process of identifying uncertainty that could affect organizational objectives, analyzing its likelihood and impact, selecting a response, and monitoring the remaining risk.

A useful relationship is:

**Threat → Vulnerability → Likelihood → Impact → Risk**

Risk decisions should support business objectives rather than attempt to eliminate every possible threat.

## 2. Risk Identification

Organizations identify assets, threats, vulnerabilities, existing controls, dependencies, and potential consequences. Sources include vulnerability assessments, audits, incident history, threat intelligence, business owners, architecture reviews, and third-party assessments.

## 3. Qualitative Risk Analysis

Qualitative analysis uses categories such as low, moderate, and high rather than relying primarily on numerical monetary values. A risk matrix may combine likelihood and impact to help prioritize decisions.

## 4. Quantitative Risk Analysis

Quantitative analysis attempts to express risk using numerical values. Common concepts include **Asset Value (AV)**, **Exposure Factor (EF)**, **Single Loss Expectancy (SLE)**, **Annualized Rate of Occurrence (ARO)**, and **Annualized Loss Expectancy (ALE)**.

**SLE = AV × EF**

**ALE = SLE × ARO**

These models support financial analysis but depend on the quality of the assumptions and data.

## 5. Risk Responses

- **Avoid:** stop the activity creating the risk.
- **Mitigate:** reduce likelihood or impact through controls.
- **Transfer:** shift some financial or contractual consequences to another party.
- **Accept:** formally acknowledge the remaining risk.

## 6. Risk Register

A risk register records identified risks, owners, affected assets, likelihood, impact, treatment, current status, deadlines, and residual risk. It supports consistent oversight and accountability.

## 7. Inherent and Residual Risk

**Inherent risk** exists before considering controls. **Residual risk** remains after controls are implemented. The organization should compare residual risk against its risk appetite and tolerance.

## 8. Security+ Exam Focus

Understand the difference between likelihood and impact. A rare event with catastrophic consequences can still require attention. Risk acceptance should be deliberate and documented, not simply the result of failing to remediate.

## Key Takeaways

Risk management is continuous. Organizations identify, analyze, treat, monitor, and communicate risk as technology, threats, business requirements, and controls change.
