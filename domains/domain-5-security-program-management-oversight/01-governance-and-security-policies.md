# Governance and Security Policies

## 1. Governance

Security governance establishes how an organization directs, controls, and oversees information security. Governance connects business objectives with security requirements and defines accountability, authority, decision-making, and oversight.

Governance answers questions such as: Who is responsible for security decisions? What level of risk is acceptable? Which requirements must the organization follow? How is compliance measured? How are exceptions approved?

## 2. Policies, Standards, Procedures, and Guidelines

A **policy** is a high-level mandatory statement of management intent. It establishes what must be done.

A **standard** provides mandatory, specific requirements that support a policy. For example, a password policy may require a defined authentication standard.

A **procedure** gives step-by-step instructions for performing a task consistently.

A **guideline** provides recommended practices and is generally more flexible than a policy or standard.

### Example Hierarchy

**Policy → Standard → Procedure → Guideline**

The policy might require secure access control. A standard can define MFA requirements. A procedure can explain how administrators enroll an employee in MFA. A guideline can recommend secure handling practices for exceptional situations.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

Security responsibilities can involve executives, the board, security leadership, IT, legal, privacy teams, HR, system owners, data owners, custodians, and users. Clear ownership prevents security responsibilities from becoming ambiguous.

## 4. Security Policy Lifecycle

Policies should be developed, approved, communicated, implemented, reviewed, updated, and retired when necessary. A policy that exists only as a document but is not communicated or enforced does not provide effective governance.

## 5. Exception Management

Organizations sometimes cannot immediately meet a security requirement. An exception process should document the reason, affected systems, risk, compensating controls, responsible owner, approval, expiration date, and review requirements.

## 6. Security+ Exam Focus

Distinguish governance documents. Policy expresses management intent; standards define mandatory requirements; procedures explain how to perform tasks; guidelines provide recommendations.

## Key Takeaways

Governance establishes accountability and direction. Policies create mandatory expectations, while standards and procedures translate those expectations into implementable requirements and actions.
