# Privacy and Data Governance

## 1. Privacy

Privacy concerns the appropriate collection, use, storage, sharing, retention, and disposal of information about individuals. Privacy requirements vary by jurisdiction, industry, data type, and contractual obligations.

## 2. Data Governance

Data governance establishes ownership, accountability, handling requirements, quality expectations, lifecycle controls, and decision-making for organizational data.

## 3. Data Classification

Classification assigns data categories according to sensitivity and business impact. Common categories include public, internal, confidential, and restricted, although organizations define their own classification scheme.

Classification determines handling requirements such as access, encryption, transmission, storage, retention, and destruction.

## 4. Data Owners and Custodians

A **data owner** is accountable for the data and makes decisions about classification, access, and protection requirements. A **data custodian** performs operational tasks such as storage, backup, access implementation, and technical protection.

## 5. Data Lifecycle

A typical lifecycle is:

**Create/collect → store → use → share/process → archive/retain → securely dispose**

Security controls should apply throughout the lifecycle rather than only when data is stored.

## 6. Data Minimization

Data minimization means collecting and retaining only information that is necessary for a legitimate purpose. Reducing unnecessary data reduces exposure and the potential impact of a breach.

## 7. Privacy by Design

Privacy considerations should be incorporated into system and process design from the beginning. Examples include minimizing collection, limiting access, providing appropriate transparency, and establishing retention and deletion mechanisms.

## 8. Security+ Exam Focus

Know the difference between data classification and ownership. Classification describes sensitivity and handling requirements; ownership establishes accountability and decision authority.

## Key Takeaways

Protect data according to its sensitivity and lifecycle. Privacy is not merely a legal concern; it affects architecture, access control, retention, monitoring, incident response, and vendor management.
